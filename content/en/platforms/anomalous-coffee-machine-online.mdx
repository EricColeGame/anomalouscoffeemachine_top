## What Is Anomalous Coffee Machine?

Anomalous Coffee Machine is a short interactive visual novel published by the developer **HoruBrain** on itch.io. It blends dialogue-driven visual-novel pacing with a light simulation loop: you stand across from a mysterious girl, you interact with a coffee machine that clearly does not follow normal rules, and the story reacts to what you do.

The title is doing a lot of work. Something "anomalous" is irregular, off-script, and inconsistent with the norm — and that is precisely the tone the game chases. You are not brewing espresso. You are poking at a system that answers back.

### The Premise in Plain Terms

You encounter a mysterious girl and an unusual machine. Conversation follows. Choices follow. The machine's responses shift based on how you engage with it, and the scene escalates in directions that community reports describe as surreal, unsettling, and occasionally darkly funny.

## Choices, Endings, and What Actually Changes

Anomalous Coffee Machine is a visual novel, which means your agency is expressed through dialogue and interaction choices rather than skill checks or combat. Community reports consistently describe multiple outcomes depending on how you treat the girl and how you handle the machine — but there is no widely agreed, developer-published ending checklist, so treat any "complete endings list" you find online with a healthy amount of skepticism.

| Decision Type | Typical Effect | Player Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational tone | Shifts how the girl responds to you | Stay consistent within a run for cleaner results |
| Interaction with the machine | Changes what the machine produces or reveals | Try the option that feels wrong at least once |
| Order of actions | Can lock in or delay certain scenes | Slow down; the game rewards attention, not speed |
| Replay behavior | Unlocks alternate readings of the same scene | Replay immediately while details are fresh |

If you are hunting for every branch, the honest advice is process-of-elimination: change one decision at a time between runs. Changing three variables at once tells you nothing about which one mattered.
