The coffee machine in HoruBrain's *Anomalous Coffee Machine* doesn't care what you order — it cares what you type. Behind the quiet visual novel pacing and the mysterious girl across the counter sits a text box that accepts far more than drink names, and players have spent a long time feeding it strange phrases just to see what comes back. ## What Are Anomalous Coffee Machine Secret Words?

*Anomalous Coffee Machine* is a short interactive visual novel and simulation-style game by HoruBrain, available on the developer's [official Anomalous Coffee Machine page on itch.io](https://horubrain.itch.io/anomalous-coffee-machine). You stand across from a mysterious girl, operate an unusual coffee machine, and watch how she responds to whatever you serve. The twist is that the machine reads language rather than a fixed menu.

Secret words are the inputs that aren't drinks. They're the names, moods, objects, feelings, and oddball phrases the machine recognizes and answers with a unique reaction, a visual change, or a story branch you'd otherwise never reach. Some are obvious the moment you think sideways about coffee. Others are hiding in the game's own dialogue, waiting for someone to type them back in.

The community treats these inputs like collectibles, which is why word lists get passed around in itch.io comments, Discord servers, and video comment sections. That's also why so many of those lists are incomplete, outdated, or written for the wrong game entirely.

## How the Hidden Word System Works

The system is simpler than it looks. Every attempt is a small experiment: you type something, the machine produces a cup, and the girl reacts. There is no timer pressure in the usual sense, and player experience suggests there is no penalty for a wrong guess — the machine simply rejects or ignores nonsense input.

That low-risk loop is exactly why hunting for Anomalous Coffee Machine secret words is fun rather than punishing. You can be wrong fifty times and lose nothing but a few seconds.

| Input type | What you type | Typical machine response |
| Standard drink | A common beverage name | A normal cup and an expected reaction |
| Ingredient | A single flavor or food word | A modified drink that shifts her mood |
| Abstract concept | A feeling, weather, or idea | An unusual cup with a scripted line |
| Name or title | A proper noun or role | A recognition line or a callback joke |
| Nonsense string | Random letters | Usually nothing at all |
| Hidden phrase | A specific secret word | A unique scene, flag, or easter egg |

Two mechanics matter most. First, some inputs appear to set flags that only pay off later in the conversation, so a word that seems to do nothing early may matter after you've progressed. Second, reactions are often visual as much as verbal — a change in the drink's color, her expression, or the framing of the scene is frequently the real signal that you found something.

## The Main Categories of Secret Words

Rather than memorizing a list that may not match your build, it helps to think in categories. Community reports consistently group the hidden inputs into a handful of recognizable families, and once you know the families, you can generate your own candidates.

| Category | Theme of the words | What it usually unlocks |
| Beverage & barista slang | Coffee-adjacent vocabulary and ordering terms | Flavor variations and shop-talk reactions |
| Ingredients & flavors | Sweet, bitter, spicy, and texture words | Mood shifts and visual drink changes |
| Emotions & moods | Feelings and states of mind | Introspective dialogue and tone changes |

The practical takeaway: if a word fits neatly into one of those families and the game's dialogue has hinted at it, it's worth testing. If it's random keyboard mashing, skip it.

## How to Find and Test Secret Words Yourself

Blind guessing is the slowest possible method. The players who fill out their collections fastest tend to combine a few approaches, and they weight them by how trustworthy each source actually is.

| Method | Reliability | Notes |
| Reading in-game text closely | High | The game frequently hints at words before you need them |
| Themed brute force with a word list | High | Slow but thorough; great for one category at a time |
| Community word lists | Medium | Often accurate but may be outdated or for the sequel |
| Streamer and VOD hunting | Medium | Reliable, but heavy on spoilers |
| Pure random typing | Low | Burns time with almost no payoff |

A repeatable testing routine beats raw enthusiasm. Work through one category at a time, keep sessions short, and write down what you tried so you don't retest the same word three weeks later.

| Step | Action | Why it helps |
| 1 | Pick one category and list 10–15 candidate words | Keeps testing focused instead of scattershot |
| 2 | Test one word at a time, then reset | Isolates the exact cause of a reaction |
| 3 | Watch the drink, not just the dialogue | Visual changes often signal a hidden trigger |
| 4 | Note the exact spelling you used | Spacing and punctuation can change results |
| 5 | Re-test old words after story progress | Some inputs only fire later in the conversation |
| 6 | Screenshot anything unusual | Proof helps you and helps other players |

One more tip that saves real time: verify which game a list belongs to before you use it. *Anomalous Coffee Machine 2* is a separate release, and community reports suggest that word lists are not reliably interchangeable between the two. A phrase that works in one may do nothing in the other.

## Tracking Discoveries, Updates, and Community Reports

If you're serious about finding every hidden input, a simple log is worth more than a great memory. The machine's reactions are subtle, and after a dozen attempts they blur together.

| Column | What to record | Example entry |
| Word tested | Exact spelling and capitalization | *as typed* |
| Date | When you tested it | Your session date |
| Build | Which game and current release | Game title plus your installed version |
| Reaction | What changed on screen | Drink color, new line, scene shift |
| Status | Confirmed, unconfirmed, or debunked | Community-confirmed vs. personal find |

Treat that log as a living document. When the developer updates the game, previously dead words can start working, and previously working words can change. Anything you haven't retested since an update should be labeled unconfirmed rather than presented as fact.

Be equally careful with what you read. Label community-sourced information as player experience or community reports, and separate it from what you've verified yourself. A word list posted in a comment thread six months ago is a lead, not a guarantee. Cross-checking two or three independent sources is the fastest way to filter out guesses that were never real.

## FAQ

**Are Anomalous Coffee Machine secret words case-sensitive?**
There's no confirmed rule that applies across every build. The safest habit is to type a word exactly as you found it, then test a lowercase variant separately. If both produce the same reaction, capitalization doesn't matter for that word.

**Do secret words from the first game work in the sequel?**
Treat them as separate games. *Anomalous Coffee Machine 2* is its own release, and community reports suggest the input lists are not reliably interchangeable. Always check which title a list was written for before you spend an evening testing it.

**Will typing the wrong word break my save or lock me out of an ending?**
Player experience suggests no. The machine generally rejects or ignores unrecognized input, and there's no reported penalty for a wrong guess. Testing is low-risk, which is exactly why it's worth doing thoroughly.

**Where do people share new Anomalous Coffee Machine secret words?**
The most active places are the comment sections on the developer's itch.io pages, community Discord servers, and comment threads under gameplay videos. Read several sources, label what's unverified, and confirm anything important in your own playthrough before you pass it along.

The short version: the machine rewards curiosity and patience far more than luck. Read the dialogue, think in categories, test one word at a time, and keep a log you can trust. Do that, and the hidden layer of *Anomalous Coffee Machine* stops being a rumor mill and starts being something you can actually finish.
